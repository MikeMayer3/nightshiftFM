# M6 — patchboard connections

M6 implementation is ready for playtesting. Human acceptance is **NOT RUN**.
This is the next milestone after M5; M7 has not started. Earlier human gameplay
gates remain unverified. The engine is still Godot **4.7.2 standard**.

## Play

Choose **New mission**, select equipment, then choose **Go live**. The patchboard
opens before wave 1 and between cleared waves. Select one of the two slots and
connect a ready recipe. Ready recipes appear first; all eight recipes remain
visible with their equipment and branch prerequisites. Empty slots are allowed.
**Go live →** starts the next wave after its two-second countdown.

Two distinct recipes may share an endpoint. They never consume support slots.
You can unplug or replace a connection only at a waiting intermission. A Signal
upgrade earned during combat still pauses the fight for one upgrade; it does not
permit rewiring. Pause and backgrounding preserve both screens.

M6 exposes the patchboard immediately for prototype testing. The campaign unlock
after mission 2 belongs to the later progression milestone. Discovery is recorded
when a connected recipe first triggers and committed at the next run checkpoint.
It survives new missions; equipped connections and their counters do not.

## Burst controls (0.6.1)

Tap **Burst** for an automatic shot at the current target. To choose a group,
press Burst, drag onto the battlefield until the aiming ring surrounds the group,
and release. Drag back off the battlefield to cancel. Direct battlefield aiming
also works. Burst retains its existing five-second cooldown and damage rules.

The 0.6.0 button accepted taps only despite its “Aim and release” label. This
patch adds button-origin gestures and explicit native touch ownership, including
secondary-finger rejection and cancellation. See [touch verification](M6_TOUCH_FIX.md).

## Authored contracts

The eight immutable resources in `content/synergies/` are generated by
`tools/generate_patchboard_content.py`. Loaded resources never store run state.
All cooldowns are shared per recipe, not per target or endpoint. Timers freeze
while paused, drafting or waiting for the player to leave the patchboard.

| Recipe | Trigger and payoff | Bound |
|---|---|---|
| Ball Lightning | Three direct Arc hits on already Charged enemies inside a live Well cause a discharge centered on the last target | 18 base damage, radius 120, six living enemies, 1.5s cooldown |
| Dead Zone | A direct Bass pulse against targets with a Net-sourced slow applies Jammed | Six targets, 0.45s jam, 0.5s recipe cooldown; elite jam is at most 0.2s with a separate 4s interruption cooldown |
| B-Side | An Echo packet prefers marked candidates within its replay reach, honoring any distinct-target rule | One selection per replay, 0.08s cooldown; precision/volley/beam geometry and native weapon caps remain intact |
| Live Wire | A Net tick applies one Charged stack to nearby enemies | Six enemies per second, three-stack status cap; creates no Arc attack |
| Pressure Drop | At direct Bass activation, enemies in the overlap of the Bass area and live Wells provide one grouping bonus | Each actor counts once even with overlapping Wells; six actors, 8% of Bass damage per actor, maximum 48%, 0.8s cooldown |
| Double Drop | Every third eligible direct Bass activation adds an immediate weaker damage repeat | 40% of Bass damage, six enemies, 0.8s cooldown; activations during cooldown do not advance the counter |
| Needle Thread | A main attack aimed at a mark gains one chassis-compatible benefit | Pulse: one extra pierced target; Burst: +20 armor penetration; Sweep: +10% damage; 0.08s cooldown; never inherited by recorded echoes |
| Feedback Loop | A real shield break emits an Arc retaliation pulse from the station | 28 base damage, radius 320, eight actors including projectiles, 12s cooldown; cannot heal, reflect or cause another break pulse |

B-Side and Needle Thread require Needle Swarm's **Marking Pins** branch. Dead Zone
requires the Net to retain a positive slow. Other recipes need their two equipped
endpoints. Eligibility is checked again on activation, so a stale connection
cannot operate with missing gear. A resisted Dead Zone still gives the existing
0.6s, 20% ability-speed reduction; it cannot permanently stop an elite's ability.

Damage pulses inherit the triggering root attack ID (or allocate one if a break
has no root), use the recipe ID as their source, have generation depth 1 and zero
eligible trigger flags. They cannot enter the direct Arc, Bass, Net or main
execution hooks. No recipe subscribes to the combat signal, so rewiring cannot
leave abandoned listeners. Effects execute synchronously within finite target
loops; M6 adds no delayed entity queue and never drops damage to hide load.

The results contribution report separates generated recipe damage from assisted
weapon damage. Assisted damage is the effective output of the assisted attack,
not an estimate of incremental bonus damage, and is already included under its
weapon. Jam seconds mean granted interruption duration, not a measured enemy
ability-time counter. Trigger, interrupt and projectile-intercept counts are
shown separately. Placeholder pulse colors and existing status tells identify
new effects; final art is outside this milestone.

## Checkpoints and compatibility

New runs use content version `m6.1`. M3/M4/M5 Continue retains its original content
rules, including timed intermissions for older saves. Profile schema 2 adds only
discovered recipe IDs; schema 1 migrates with an empty discovery list.

M6 checkpoints preserve the two slots, intermission hold, partial trigger counts,
cooldowns and contribution totals alongside the existing actors, effects, draft
and independent RNG streams. Invalid/duplicate/unavailable recipe IDs, missing
status capabilities, malformed counters, out-of-range cooldowns and inconsistent
waiting phases are rejected. Rewiring clears partial counters but retains
cooldowns to prevent unplug/replug abuse. Saves still use the atomic temporary
write and recoverable backup. No new cloud or native-service dependency exists.

## Reproduction

From the project directory, set:

```sh
export GODOT_BIN=/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot
export JAVA_HOME='/Applications/Android Studio.app/Contents/jbr/Contents/Home'
export ANDROID_HOME=/Users/michaelmayer/Library/Android/sdk
sh tools/godot.sh import
sh tools/godot.sh test
python3 tools/check_foundation.py
sh tools/godot.sh smoke
"$GODOT_BIN" --path . --script res://tests/visual_patchboard.gd
"$GODOT_BIN" --headless --path . --script res://tests/patchboard_stress.gd
"$GODOT_BIN" --headless --path . --script res://tests/patchboard_balance.gd
sh tools/godot.sh android-debug
python3 tools/build_patchboard_playtest.py --output builds/android/nightshift-m6-qa.apk
```

The regular debug APK is `builds/android/nightshift-m6.apk`, Android **0.6.0/code 9**.
The isolated QA APK uses `org.nightshiftfm.patchboardqa`; only its Android package
and app label differ. Its regular boot scene and runtime sources match the main
project. The build script writes the runtime source hashes and APK hash beside
it. Physical phone installation and iOS M6 checks are **NOT RUN**.

## Evidence and remaining acceptance

Evidence is in `docs/evidence/M6/`. Regression covers all eight real triggers and
inactive cases, requirements, cooldowns, generation exclusions, compatible main
benefits, elite fallback, 100 rewires, reset, migration, truncated-save recovery,
actual screen controls and a complete run's decision/checkpoint restoration.
All 1,922 regression checks and seven foundation checks passed, including a
clean temporary source copy with a fresh import and startup smoke. Android
0.6.0/code 9 exported and both APK signatures verified. The isolated emulator
passed actual touch wiring, duplicate prevention, unplug/reconnect, wave start,
force-stop/Continue of both wiring and a live Signal draft, and one accepted
recovered choice while retaining its connection. Its package remains at the menu;
the regular emulator app and physical phone were untouched. These are early-flow
emulator checks, not a full Android mission.

Desktop verification uses viewport mouse input for wiring and starting combat,
with screenshots at 360×640, 450×800 and 450×950.

The stress fixture tests all 28 distinct recipe pairs with legal equipment and
rank-8 paths. Each begins with 150 stationary enemies and 400 enemy projectiles,
then runs ten simulated seconds with automatic attacks and adversarial shield
break/reflection attempts. Large artificial enemy health keeps the encounter
busy. This is a CPU/event-bound fixture, not a physical-device FPS or save fixture.
Peak pending arsenal effects: **11**; maximum recipe damage hits per root: **12**;
maximum observed event count: **1,437**; slowest fixture: about **505 ms CPU** for
600 steps. No malformed generation or unbounded work was observed.

Nine documented simulations (three main chassis for each of charge/control,
group/repeats, and marked/replays) completed with nine victories. They use a
strong automated aiming and build-selection policy with seed 11, not random
human behavior. Both intended recipes triggered for all three build families.
These demonstrate implementation reachability and retain every accepted choice;
they do not establish difficulty, fun, or human win rates.

Human-test the three combinations, record whether their strengths and UI match
expectations, and assess intermission pacing on phones. **Human playtests,
physical-device usability, sustained phone performance and final art acceptance
remain NOT RUN.** Stop at M6.
